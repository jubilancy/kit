## Executive Summaries & Reports {#executive-summaries-&-reports}

### 1\. Quarterly Business Review (QBR) Deck Outline

**Prompt:**

```
I need to prepare a QBR presentation for [Q1/Q2/Q3/Q4] for my [department name] team. We report to [CEO/SVP/Board]. Here are the raw numbers and notes from the quarter:

[Paste your metrics, wins, misses, key initiatives, and any raw notes]

Create a QBR deck outline with these sections: (1) Executive Summary — 3 bullet max on where we stand vs. plan, (2) Key Metrics vs. Targets — table format showing metric, target, actual, and delta, (3) Top 3 Wins with business impact quantified, (4) Top 3 Misses with root cause and corrective action, (5) Key Initiatives for Next Quarter with owners and milestones, (6) Resource Requests or Escalations. Keep the language crisp and executive-ready. No fluff — every slide should answer "so what?"
```

### 2\. Weekly Executive Status Update

**Prompt:**

```
Write a concise weekly status update email for [my CEO/the leadership team]. I lead the [department] team of [number] people. Here's what happened this week:

[Paste raw notes — what shipped, what's blocked, key decisions, metrics movements, people updates]

Format it as: (1) TL;DR — one sentence on the single most important thing, (2) Wins — 3-4 bullets, quantified where possible, (3) Risks & Blockers — anything that could derail next week, with proposed mitigation, (4) Key Decisions Needed — if I need alignment or approval on anything, state it clearly. Total length under 250 words. Tone: confident, data-driven, no hedging language.
```

### 3\. Board-Ready Executive Summary from Raw Data

**Prompt:**

```
I need to turn the following raw data and notes into a board-ready executive summary for [company name]. The board meets [monthly/quarterly] and expects a concise narrative, not a data dump.

[Paste: revenue figures, customer metrics, burn rate, headcount, product milestones, pipeline numbers, any notable events]

Write a 1-page executive summary (under 500 words) structured as: (1) Business Performance — how the company is tracking against plan, (2) Key Highlights — 3-4 achievements worth calling out, (3) Challenges & Risks — be honest but pair each risk with what we're doing about it, (4) Outlook — what the next 90 days look like. Use confident, precise language. Board members have 10 minutes to read this before the meeting — make every word count.
```

### 4\. Post-Mortem Summary for a Failed Initiative

**Prompt:**

```
I need to write a post-mortem report for a [product launch/campaign/initiative] called [name] that did not hit its goals. Here are the details:

- Original goal: [what we expected to achieve]
- Actual result: [what actually happened]
- Timeline: [when it started, key milestones, when we pulled the plug or declared it a miss]
- Team involved: [roles, not names]
- My raw notes on what went wrong: [paste your honest assessment]

Write a blame-free post-mortem structured as: (1) Objective & Expected Outcome, (2) What Actually Happened (timeline of key decisions), (3) Root Causes (be specific — not "communication issues" but what actually broke down), (4) What We'd Do Differently, (5) Recommended Next Steps. Tone: honest, constructive, focused on systemic improvements not individual failures.
```

### 5\. Monthly Department Metrics Dashboard Narrative

**Prompt:**

```
I manage the [department] team and I need to write the narrative that accompanies our monthly metrics dashboard. My audience is [CEO/CFO/leadership team]. Here are this month's numbers:

[Paste: KPIs with current values, month-over-month change, and target. Include any context for anomalies.]

(2) Calls out the 2-3 metrics that matter most and explains the "why" behind any significant moves, (3) Flags any leading indicators that suggest next month could be different, (4) Ends with 1-2 action items we're taking based on the data. Don't just restate the numbers — interpret them. My boss wants to know what the data means, not what the data says.

## Strategic Planning & Decision Making {#strategic-planning-&-decision-making}

### 11\. Decision Framework for a Major Investment

**Prompt:**

```
I'm evaluating whether to [describe the decision — e.g., "invest $500K in building an in-house data team vs. continuing to use our agency" / "expand into the European market" / "acquire a smaller competitor"]. I'm the [your title] at [company name] in [industry].

Help me build a structured decision framework. Include: (1) Decision Criteria — the 5-7 factors that matter most (cost, speed, risk, strategic fit, team capability, etc.), weighted by importance, (2) Option Analysis — evaluate [Option A] vs. [Option B] (vs. [Option C] if applicable) against each criterion, (3) Risk Assessment — what's the worst case for each option and how recoverable is it, (4) Reversibility — how easy is it to change course if we pick wrong, (5) Recommendation — based on the analysis, what would you recommend and why. Force me to think about second-order effects, not just the obvious pros/cons.
```

### 15\. Scenario Planning for Economic Uncertainty

**Prompt:**

```
I'm the [your title] at [company name]. [Describe the uncertainty — e.g., "We're heading into a potential recession" / "A major customer representing 30% of revenue is at risk of churning" / "New regulation could impact our core product"]. I need to prepare our leadership team with scenario plans.

Build three scenarios: (1) Best Case — what happens if things go better than expected, what we should be ready to capitalize on, (2) Base Case — most likely outcome, what we should plan for and the adjustments needed, (3) Worst Case — what happens if it goes badly, and the specific levers we can pull (cost cuts, pivots, cash preservation) with a trigger point for each action. For each scenario, include: revenue impact estimate, headcount implications, strategic shifts required, and the first 3 actions we'd take. End with a recommendation on which scenario to plan against and why.
```

### 17\. Strategic Offsite Agenda

**Prompt:**

```
I'm organizing a [1-day/2-day] leadership offsite for [number] senior leaders at [company name]. The goals of the offsite are: [e.g., "align on 2027 strategy" / "resolve ongoing tension between sales and product" / "define our AI strategy" / "figure out how to grow from $10M to $50M ARR"].

Design a detailed agenda that includes: (1) Session titles, descriptions, time blocks, and who facilitates each, (2) A mix of strategic discussion, working sessions (not just presentations), and decision-making time, (3) At least one session that forces honest conversation about what's NOT working, (4) Clear outputs for each session — what should we leave with? (5) Suggested pre-work so people come prepared, not cold. Avoid: death by PowerPoint, vague "brainstorming" sessions with no structure, and any session over 90 minutes without a break. ### 18\. Kill/Keep Decision for an Underperforming Product

**Prompt:**

```
I need to decide whether to keep investing in or shut down [product/feature/service name] at [company name]. It's been live for [timeframe]. Here's the situation:

- Original thesis: [why we built/launched it]
- Current performance: [revenue, users, engagement, or whatever metrics matter]
- Resources allocated: [headcount, spend]
- Team sentiment: [how the team working on it feels]
- Customer feedback: [what customers say]
- Sunk cost: [what we've already invested]

Help me think through this clearly. Structure it as: (1) Is the original thesis still valid? What's changed? (2) What would "success" need to look like in the next 6 months to justify continued investment? (3) Opportunity cost — what else could this team/budget be doing? (4) Exit options — if we kill it, what's the cleanest way to do it (sunset timeline, customer migration, team redeployment)? (5) Your recommendation. Help me avoid sunk cost fallacy but also avoid killing something too early.
```

## Meeting Management & Communication {#meeting-management-&-communication}

### 21\. All-Hands / Town Hall Talking Points

**Prompt:**

```
I'm giving an all-hands presentation to my [department / entire company] of [number] people. The key themes I want to cover are:

[List your topics — e.g., "Q3 results, upcoming reorg, new product launch, hiring update, addressing morale concerns about layoffs at competitors"]

Write my talking points for a [15/30/45]-minute all-hands. Structure: (1) Opening — set the tone, acknowledge the current mood, don't start with slides, (2) Business Update — the numbers, but framed as a story not a spreadsheet, (3) Strategic Priorities — what matters most right now and why, (4) Hard Topics — if there's something uncomfortable to address, give me the language to do it honestly without creating panic, (5) Wins & Recognition — specific callouts I should make (I'll fill in names), (6) Close — what I want people to walk away feeling and doing. Tone: authentic, direct, human. Not corporate-speak.
```

### 23\. Executive Email Announcing a Difficult Decision

**Prompt:**

```
I need to write an email to [my team / the entire company / my department] announcing [describe the difficult decision — e.g., "we're sunsetting a product line" / "we're doing a reduction in force" / "we're cutting a popular perk" / "we're restructuring the team" / "a key leader is leaving"].

Context: [explain the reasoning, what led to this, who's affected, what happens next]

Write the email following these principles: (1) Lead with the decision — don't bury it under 3 paragraphs of context, (2) Explain the "why" honestly — people can handle hard truths better than corporate platitudes, (3) Acknowledge the human impact — if this hurts people, say so, (4) Be specific about what happens next — timelines, support, who to contact, (5) Close with appropriate tone — not falsely optimistic, but grounded in what we're building toward. Under 400 words. No "excited to announce" — this isn't exciting, it's necessary.
```

### 30\. Post-Meeting Decision Summary

**Prompt:**

```
I just had a [30/60/90]-minute meeting with [list attendees and their roles]. Here are my rough notes:

[Paste raw, messy notes — what was discussed, what people said, where there was disagreement, what was decided]

Turn this into a clean decision summary I can send to all attendees and relevant stakeholders within 30 minutes. Format: (1) Meeting Context — one sentence on the purpose, (2) Decisions Made — numbered list, each decision stated clearly and unambiguously (this is the most important section — vague decisions cause months of confusion), (3) Open Items — things we discussed but didn't resolve, with a clear owner and deadline to resolve each, (4) Action Items — who does what by when (bold the owner's name), (5) Next Steps — when we reconvene, if needed. If any decision in my notes sounds ambiguous, flag it and suggest clearer language. Most execution problems start with people leaving meetings thinking different things were decided.

## Team Leadership & Performance Reviews {#team-leadership-&-performance-reviews}

### 31\. Performance Review Draft for a Strong Performer

**Prompt:**

```
I need to write a performance review for [direct report's role] who is a strong performer this cycle. Review period: [timeframe]. Here are my raw notes on their performance:

- Key accomplishments: [list them]
- Metrics/impact: [quantified results]
- Strengths demonstrated: [what they do well]
- Growth areas I've observed: [where they can improve]
